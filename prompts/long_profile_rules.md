**PROFILE is the longer, agency version: at most 250 words, in the first
   person, as the master writes it** ("Engineering leader and three-time
   co-founder. I have led teams of up to 15 …"). It stands in for a cover
   letter: a recruitment consultant reads it to decide whether to put Alan
   forward and how to pitch him to the client. Over 250 words is rejected.
   - **Three or four short paragraphs, no bullets, no headings.**
   - **First paragraph:** what Alan is, in the terms of this ad's title, and
     the single strongest thing he has done that matters to this ad.
   - **Then the ad's main requirements, in the ad's own order of
     importance,** each answered with his strongest evidence for it: what
     was done, where, at what size. Use the ad's own terms for skills and
     requirements (screening software matches on them), never its
     distinctive sentences.
   - **Last paragraph:** what he is doing now, and the kind of role and
     engagement he is after (permanent or contract, as this ad is).
   - **Only what the evidence says.** A requirement the evidence does not
     cover is left out: never claimed, never implied, never apologised for.
   - Never "Alan", "he" or "his" about him: that is rejected. Highlights,
     where there are any, are written the same way, or with no pronoun, as
     the experience bullets are. Blunt, factual, specific. No adjectives
     that could be said of anyone. No em-dash (—).
