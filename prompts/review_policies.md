# review_policies — v1

The written policies every application document must meet. The reviewer
checks against these; it is never shown the instructions the writer was given.

## Hard rules

1. **No invented experience.** Every claim must be something the candidate has
   done. A fact that appears nowhere else in the documents and is not plausibly
   part of the stated roles is a finding. Two people collapsed into one, a
   result moved to a different employer, or a bigger number than stated
   elsewhere in the same documents are findings.
2. **No sycophancy.** No "thrilled", "passionate", "excited to apply", no
   flattering the company, no enthusiasm in place of evidence.
3. **Blunt, factual, engineering register.** Short sentences. Specific claims.
4. **Nothing is submitted.** The documents are drafts for a human to send.

## The age-signal policy

Never state or compute a career length: no years-of-experience figures, no
"since <year>", no decades, no "over 20 years". Dates beside roles are fine;
arithmetic on them is not.

## Fit to the role

- The first thing a reader meets — the resume's PROFILE paragraph, the first
  CAREER HIGHLIGHT, the letter's opening — must be the evidence this kind of
  role is hired for. For a people-focused role that is growing people: hiring,
  coaching, team building. Opening such a role on a performance exit, a
  disagreement, or AI tooling is a finding.
- Every must-have in the ad that the documents can answer should be answered
  on the resume itself, not only in the letter.

## Story reuse

One story appears at most twice on a resume (a highlight and one experience
bullet, never also the PROFILE paragraph), at most once in a letter, and at
most once across one form's answers. More is a finding: it reads as a
candidate with one story.

## Phrasing

No defensive or self-referential phrasing: "not a claim", "not advisory",
"this ad is asking for", "your ad asks for … in practice", or more than one
"not X but Y" construction in a document.

## Standing decisions — do not report these as faults

- A letter names one gap honestly, in one sentence. That is deliberate.
- Founder ventures in EARLIER CAREER show their years, e.g. "(2006–16)".
- The resume's format, section order and two-to-three-page length are fixed.
